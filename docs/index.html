A platform that moves like a ship at sea

Three stepper-driven legs reproduce the heave, pitch and roll of a vessel in sea states 1 to 4. The traces below come from the same wave generator the firmware runs.

Six modes in one program

One menu on the serial port or in the control panel. The key is what you press.

KeyModeWhat it does
1SetupHomes the legs, then you jog each leg until the platform is level and mark its minimum. Jog all three together and mark the maximum. Save to flash so the numbers survive a power cycle.
2Heave cycleAll legs rise from minimum to maximum and back, pausing at each end. Pause, speed and the length of the end pause change while it moves.
3Pitch and roll circlesThe platform tilts in a slow circle, with or without heave. Size, period and direction change live.
4WavesIrregular waves for sea states 1 to 4. Press 1 to 4 to change sea state while it runs, r for a new random sea.
5MAX platformThe same waves, exaggerated until they use the full travel. If the motors cannot follow at normal speed, the wave clock slows down. d sweeps heave, pitch and roll through their whole range in turn.
6ManualSet heave in millimetres, pitch and roll in degrees: step with keys or type a value such as h12.5.

q goes back to the menu from any mode and x stops everything at once. At the menu, g sends every leg back to rest (the minimums) and h homes again.

Sea states 1 to 4

Each sea is a sum of 24 sine waves drawn from a Bretschneider spectrum, with the vessel following the wave slope for pitch and roll.

The heights shown are scaled as Hs4 × (Hs / Hs4)0.75, so sea states 1 and 2 stay visible next to sea state 4. Set WAVE_HS_EXPONENT to 1 for true proportions.

From waves to motor steps

Modes never touch the motor pins. They hand a target pose to the chain below, and every stage can only make the motion gentler.

  1. Wave generator

    Sums 24 sine waves into heave, pitch and roll.

  2. Pose limiter

    Fits the motion to the platform’s range and slows the wave clock if the legs could not follow.

  3. Inverse kinematics

    Closed-form crank angle for each leg from heave, pitch and roll.

  4. Step engine

    A 10 kHz clock follows each leg target within speed and acceleration limits and brakes before the travel limits.

  5. CL86T drivers

    STEP and DIR pulses, 2000 per motor turn through a 10:1 gearbox.

  6. Cranks and links

    20 000 steps per crank turn, 0.018° per step.

Set up and run

  1. Copy the firmware. Put the contents of firmware/Core/Inc and firmware/Core/Src into the CubeIDE project, replacing main.c and main.h. Do not regenerate code from the .ioc afterwards without checking main.c.

  2. Build and flash. If the linker cannot find sinf, add m under Linker → Libraries.

  3. Connect. Open the control panel in Chrome or Edge, or PuTTY on the ST-Link serial port at 115200, 8N1.

  4. Set up. Press 1. Homing runs first. Jog the minimums, jog the maximum, press s to save.

  5. Run a mode. Press 2 to 6. Keep a hand near the driver power switches the first time.

Wiring

LegSTEPDIRLimit switch
X (front)PA0PA1PB9
Y (back right)PB4PB5PB0
Z (back left)PA8PA9PA4

Drivers: CL86T at 2000 pulses per revolution, PUL− and DIR− to ground. Switches are normally closed with the common to ground and the internal pull-up on the pin, so a pressed or broken switch reads high. Pitch is positive when the front (Z) leg rises, roll is positive when the X side rises.

The millimetre and degree values use placeholder geometry until the real crank, link and radii are entered in plat_config.h. The legs stay inside their limits whatever the geometry says. The visualiser’s geometry panel writes the lines to paste.

What has been checked

  • The JavaScript matches the C. 461 comparisons of waves, kinematics and wave scaling against the firmware’s own output, worst wave difference 0.0001 m.
  • Kinematics round trip. Forward kinematics recovers the pose from the leg positions to within 0.002 on 1,615 reachable poses.
  • The sea has the right size. Twenty minutes of simulated waves match the analytic heave, pitch, roll and velocity statistics to within half a percent.
  • Step engine. No overshoot onto a target, never above the speed limit, follows a sine within 5 steps, and stops softly in front of a travel limit even when the target stops abruptly.
  • Whole program. A scripted session through homing, setup, saving, every mode and a power cycle ran for 735 simulated seconds with no limit violations.

All of this ran on a PC with a model of the legs. The program has not yet run on the rig. Homing and pulse timing are ported from the code that has, so check the first run with a hand on the driver power switches.
